The incident in one sentence
Every redundant path existed; nothing was watching, so nothing decided. Detection + reaction lived in a sleeping human — 55 minutes of outage for 4 minutes of typing.
Why the interface stayed green
The cut was three hops inside the MPLS cloud — infrastructure Vaultline doesn't own. FortiOS will withdraw a static route when its next-hop interface drops, but the CE-facing interfaces never dropped. Local link state proves the first hop only; remote path failure needs a protocol (someone who saw it must tell you).
Administrative boundaries drawn on purpose
The dashed boxes mark where Vaultline's administration ends. Inside them, an IGP (OSPF) can flood full topology among trusted routers. Across them — toward ISP-A, ISP-B, or the MPLS provider — only an EGP (BGP) is appropriate: reachability claims over TCP 179, no map-sharing with other companies.
What this phase will change on this diagram
  • OSPF between the core router and both FortiGates, so interior routes converge without hands (S21).
  • eBGP from FG-EDGE-P to ISP-A and FG-EDGE-DR to ISP-B, so a dead breakout withdraws itself (S22).
  • Redistribution decisions — and the AD ladder (Static 10 · eBGP 20 · OSPF 110 · iBGP 200) arbitrating any overlap.

Monday morning, one week after the post-mortem. Priya's design meeting settled the protocol — OSPF for the interior — but nothing ships to production without proof. Why a lab bench at all
Session 20's lesson was that Vaultline's interior converges only at human speed. Before OSPF touches the production core, the team proves adjacency formation, watches every neighbor state transition live, and deliberately breaks timers, areas, and interface parameters — so the first time an adjacency sticks in production, the symptom is already an old friend.
Priya's whiteboard — the target this bench is rehearsing for
OSPF interior across Vaultline: backbone (area 0) at HQ containing the core router and FG-EDGE-P; branches hanging off it. One design question deliberately left open on the whiteboard: does the DR site join area 0, or get its own area? That decision needs today's LSA-flooding-scope discussion before it can be made honestly.
