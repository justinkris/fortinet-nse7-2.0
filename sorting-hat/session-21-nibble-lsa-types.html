SESSION 21 · NIBBLE · LSA TYPES 1–5 + 7

The Six LSAs: Who Speaks, and How Far

Every LSA answers two questions — who is allowed to say this, and how far is the message allowed to travel. Answer both and the exam question answers itself.

The exam almost never asks "what is a Type 3." It asks who originates it — because the originator is where the distractors live.

Type 2 is originated by the DR, never the BDR. Type 4 by the ABR, never the ASBR. Type 7 by the ASBR, translated by the ABR. Three traps, one table.

THE TABLE · ONE ROW PER LSA

TypeNameWhat it describesOriginated byHow far it floodsThe exam trap
1Router LSA A router's autobiography — its own links and their costs EVERY ROUTER Own area only — never crosses an ABR An ASBR flags itself here with the E-bit — that flag is what triggers the ABR to generate a Type 4
2Network LSA The multi-access segment itself (the pseudonode) and everyone attached to it THE DR — ONLY Own area only Not "the DR and BDR." The BDR originates nothing — it's an understudy holding a script, not a co-author
3Summary LSA A claim, not a map: "prefix X reachable through me, cost Y" — inter-area routes ABR Injected per-area; re-originated by each ABR it crosses — hops border to border, never floods domain-wide as one object This is where OSPF turns distance-vector. Loop prevention is the backbone rule (all areas attach to area 0; Type 3s heard from a non-zero area are ignored), plus intra-area routes always beat inter-area
4ASBR Summary Directions to an ASBR that lives in another area — "reach that smuggler through me" ABR — NOT THE ASBR Into areas that can't see the ASBR The #1 LSA distractor. A router can't advertise directions to itself from an area it isn't in. Type 4 = originated by the ABR, about the ASBR. It exists for the gap between the border and the smuggler
5AS External Foreign news itself — a prefix from outside OSPF (static, BGP, RIP, connected non-OSPF) ASBR Domain-wide — the only LSA that crosses area borders unchanged, through all standard areas Banned from stub areas and NSSAs. Stub areas get a default route (as a Type 3) from their ABR instead. External metrics: type E1 = internal + external cost; E2 = external only; E1 preferred over E2
7NSSA External Foreign news born inside an NSSA — the "not-so-stubby" loophole ASBR INSIDE THE NSSA NSSA only — at the border, the NSSA ABR translates it into a Type 5 for the rest of the domain Why it exists: a stub area bans Type 5s to stay small, but what if a router inside it needs to redistribute something? NSSA = stub rules + a local envelope (7) that gets re-stamped as a 5 at the exit. Two authors, one route: ASBR writes the 7, ABR publishes the 5
Stub & NSSA in two lines: a stub area says "don't flood me the world's externals — just hand me a default route" (ABR sends the default as a Type 3; Type 5s banned). An NSSA says the same, plus "…but I have my own small ASBR, so let its externals out" — those travel as Type 7 inside, translated to Type 5 at the ABR.
Session 21 register note: the Type 4 originator was missed in this session's quiz — even after correction. If you read only one row twice, read row 4. Say it aloud: Type 4 — originated by the ABR, about the ASBR.
ONE PICTURE · THE KINGDOM

The kingdom of claims and maps

One image to reconstruct the whole table: villages (areas) around a capital (area 0), heralds at the gates, and a foreign merchant whose news travels further than any map is allowed to.

The LSA kingdom — every LSA type as a character, every flooding scope as a wall it can or cannot cross.
Flat minimalistic but highly detailed cartoon illustration, modern vector-art aesthetic, warm palette: cream background, coral accents, muted gold highlights, sage greens, soft brown outlines, clean bold line work, no gradients, no 3D. Wide landscape composition, slightly elevated three-quarter view of a medieval kingdom drawn as a network diagram. CENTER: a walled capital city labeled "AREA 0 — BACKBONE", its wall gates manned by herald characters in amber sashes labeled "ABR". Around it, three walled villages labeled "AREA 1", "AREA 2", and "AREA 51 — NSSA" (the NSSA village wall has a small mail-slot instead of an open gate), each connected to the capital by a single road — NO roads between villages (one faint, crossed-out dirt path between two villages stamped "FORBIDDEN — backbone rule"). INSIDE each village: (Type 1) every villager holds up a hand-drawn map of their own house and yard, labeled "Type 1 — my own links"; (Type 2) in the village square, ONE town crier wearing a green "DR" medal stands on a podium describing the square itself with a banner "Type 2 — the segment speaks through its DR", while a second character with a "BDR" badge sits silently on a bench holding a rolled-up copy, clearly NOT speaking. AT THE GATES: the amber ABR heralds hold up short scrolls reading "Type 3 — prefix X, through me, cost Y" — pointedly summaries, not maps; a small sign at each gate reads "maps never leave the village". FAR EDGE: outside the kingdom entirely, a foreign merchant caravan labeled "ISP / BGP / static" hands goods to a coral-cloaked smuggler character at the kingdom's edge labeled "ASBR", who launches paper airplanes labeled "Type 5 — external news" that fly OVER all the walls into every standard village (dotted flight paths crossing walls unchanged) — but bounce off the NSSA village and a small "STUB" hamlet, each protected by an umbrella labeled "no Type 5s — default route only". NEAR THE NSSA village: inside its wall, a tiny local smuggler writes on a distinctive coral envelope labeled "Type 7", passes it through the mail-slot to the amber ABR herald, who re-stamps it at a desk labeled "translated: 7 → 5" and launches it onward as a paper airplane. FINALLY: one herald at the capital holds a signpost pointing toward the far-edge smuggler, labeled "Type 4 — directions to the ASBR (written by the ABR!)". Generous spacing, every labeled object reinforcing exactly one LSA concept, educational yet playful.

Mental Note — the recall chain
1 = everyone's autobiography. 2 = the DR speaks for the square. 3 = the herald's claim at the gate. 4 = the herald's directions to the smuggler. 5 = the smuggler's paper airplane over every wall. 7 = the NSSA's envelope, re-stamped to a 5 at the exit. Maps stay home; claims cross borders; only external news flies domain-wide.
