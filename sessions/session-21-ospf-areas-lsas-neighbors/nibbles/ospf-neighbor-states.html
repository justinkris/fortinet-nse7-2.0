SESSION 21 · NIBBLE · OSPF NEIGHBOR STATES

Down to Full: A Love Story

Seven states, one relationship. A guy was DOWN to INITiate a 2-WAY... and by the end, she knew he was FULL of it.

If you can retell this comic, you can recite the entire OSPF adjacency state machine — in order, under exam pressure.

Down → Init → 2-Way → ExStart → Exchange → Loading → Full. Each panel pairs the joke with the real protocol event, plus the stuck-state that the exam loves to hide there.

THE MNEMONIC · 60-SECOND COMIC

He was DOWN to INITiate a 2-WAY...

Two routers meeting is a courtship with rules. Read the strip left to right — the amber box is the story beat, the blue box is what OSPF is actually doing, and the red line is where real adjacencies get stuck at that exact state.

He's DOWN bad — but she doesn't know he exists yet. Not one word exchanged.
No hellos received from this neighbor. Dead interval may have expired on a previously-known neighbor, dropping it back here.
Stuck here? Hellos never accepted at all: mismatched hello/dead timers, area ID, subnet/mask, or authentication. A timer mismatch means no neighbor entry ever forms.

Flat minimalistic detailed cartoon, vector style, cream background, no gradients. Comic panel: the coral-shirt guy "ROUTER A" now standing, waving enthusiastically with a big speech bubble reading "HELLO! I'm A!" containing a small envelope icon labeled "Hello packet · RID 1.1.1.1". The sage-green girl "ROUTER B" has half-turned, one eyebrow raised, holding her own speech bubble that reads "Hello, I'm B" — but crucially her bubble shows a neighbor-list clipboard that is EMPTY (his name is NOT on it, shown as a blank line with a magnifying glass hovering over it). He INITiates: "Hello!" She's heard him… but her replies don't mention his name yet. One-way conversation.
Hello received, but my own Router ID is not yet listed inside the neighbor's hello. Communication proven in one direction only.
Stuck here? Your hellos aren't reaching or aren't accepted by them — one-way filtering, ACL, or unicast/multicast delivery problem toward the neighbor.
32-WAYMutual — numbers saved

Both smiling, each holding a phone with the other's name saved. A referee appears: "Who's the DR here?"
Flat detailed cartoon, vector style, cream background. Comic panel: coral-shirt guy and sage-green girl now facing each other, both smiling, each holding up a phone; his screen shows contact card "ROUTER B ✓" and hers shows "ROUTER A ✓" — mutual recognition. Two solid arrows between them forming a clean two-way loop, labeled "your hello lists MY name". Election time."
It's officially a 2-WAY thing — she saved his number, he saved hers, and both of them know it's mutual. But who takes the lead? The room votes.
I see my own RID inside their hello. Bidirectional confirmed. On broadcast networks the DR/BDR election happens from here (wait timer = dead interval).
Stuck here? Two cases. HEALTHY: DROTHER↔DROTHER pairs stop at 2-Way forever by design. FAULT: both routers priority 0 → no one is eligible → no DR can ever exist → the lab bench bug.
4EXSTARTWho texts first?
images/s21-comic-04-exstart.png
Standing at a doorway: "After you." "No, after YOU." Higher Router ID wins and goes first.
Flat detailed cartoon, vector style, cream background. Comic panel: the pair standing at a doorway labeled "DATABASE EXCHANGE — this way", doing the polite standoff: he gestures "after you", she gestures "no, after YOU". Above them a floating scoreboard compares two jersey numbers: "RID 10.10.12.1" vs "RID 10.10.12.2" with a crown appearing over the HIGHER number, labeled "MASTER — sets the sequence number"; the other gets a ribbon labeled "SLAVE". At their feet, two identical suitcases must fit through the doorway; the door frame has a measuring tape across it labeled "MTU 1500" — a subtle hint that mismatched suitcase sizes jam this exact doorway. Caption plate: "EXSTART — decide who leads before exchanging anything."
The ExStart standoff: who texts first? They settle it like adults — biggest Router ID leads, and sets the pace of the whole conversation.
Master/slave negotiation — higher RID becomes master and picks the initial DBD sequence number. Nothing about the database has moved yet.
Stuck here? The classic: MTU mismatch — DBD packets from the big-MTU side get dropped. Also duplicate Router IDs. Exam gold: "stuck at ExStart" ≈ check MTU first.
5EXCHANGESwapping life stories
images/s21-comic-05-exchange.png
They trade photo-album INDEXES — tables of contents, not the actual photos.
Flat detailed cartoon, vector style, cream background. Comic panel: the pair now seated at the café table, sliding documents to each other. Important detail: the documents are TABLE-OF-CONTENTS pages, clearly labeled "MY LIFE — index only" and "MY LIFE — chapter list", showing bullet lists of chapter titles with page numbers, NOT actual photos or stories. Each sheet is stamped "DBD — Database Description". He reads hers with a highlighter, circling two chapter titles he's never heard of. Small ack stamps ("ACK ✓") fly back after each sheet. Caption plate: "EXCHANGE — summaries only. Headers, not the whole database." Coral, sage, muted gold accents, clean bold line work.
Now they EXCHANGE life stories — but only the chapter titles. "Here's an index of everything I know." Neither hands over the full book yet.
DBD packets flow — summaries (LSA headers) of each LSDB. Each router compares the index against its own database and notes what it's missing.
Stuck here? Usually the same suspects as ExStart — MTU or packet loss chopping the DBD conversation mid-stream.
6LOADING"Tell me EVERYTHING"
images/s21-comic-06-loading.png
She's LOADING up on the missing chapters — cross-examining every story he glossed over.
Flat detailed cartoon, vector style, cream background. Comic panel: the sage-green girl leaning forward like a detective, holding the highlighted index page and pointing at circled chapter titles, speech bubble: "Chapter 7. 'Barcelona 2019'. Full story. NOW." — her requests fly as envelopes labeled "LSR — Link State Request". The coral-shirt guy hands over thick full chapters as parcels labeled "LSU — Link State Update", sweating slightly. A progress bar floats above the table: "LSDB sync… 87%" in muted gold. A small stack of already-received chapters sits beside her with checkmarks. Caption plate: "LOADING — request the missing pieces, receive the full LSAs."
She's LOADING up on details — every chapter he glossed over, she demands in full. "You mentioned Barcelona. Tell me everything about Barcelona."
LSRs go out for every LSA that was in their index but missing (or newer) in my LSDB; LSUs come back with the complete LSAs.
Stuck here? Rare — corrupted or repeatedly-retransmitted LSAs, packet loss. Persistent Loading usually means something is mangling LSUs in transit.
7FULLShe knows everything. He's FULL of it.
images/s21-comic-07-full.png
Wide finale panel: identical "story books" side by side — she now holds a complete copy of his entire database. Verdict delivered.
Flat detailed cartoon, vector style, cream background, wide cinematic panel. CENTER: the pair standing side by side, each holding an identical thick book — both covers read "THE NETWORK — complete edition", with a large equals sign floating between the two books and a golden banner: "IDENTICAL LSDBs". She wears a detective's satisfied smirk, arms crossed, speech bubble: "I know EVERYTHING you know. You're FULL of it." He shrugs, caught, but grinning, bubble: "…and now so are you." LEFT edge: a small heart-shaped seal stamped "ADJACENCY: FULL". RIGHT edge: a tiny Dijkstra character (old-fashioned professor with a map and compass) already drawing shortest-path tree lines from the shared book onto a wall map labeled "SPF TREE". Bottom caption plate in muted gold: "FULL — databases synchronized. Now, and only now, can routes be computed." Warm cream, coral, sage, gold palette, clean bold outlines, no gradients.
And in the end she knows every single story he's got — which is exactly how she can prove he's FULL of it. The punchline is the protocol: you can only call someone's whole database "lies" once you hold a complete copy of it.
LSDBs are identical. The adjacency is Full — and only now does SPF/Dijkstra compute routes over the shared map. On broadcast segments, Full is reached only with the DR and BDR (2n−3 Full adjacencies for n routers, never the full mesh of n(n−1)/2).

Stuck-state cheat row

FULLShe knows everything — he's full of itNothing. This is the goal. Celebrate.
Lab bench flashback: our two FortiGates sat at 2-Way/DROTHER forever because both interfaces carried priority 0 — a flawless election with zero candidates. One set priority 1 on one box and the neighbor sprinted ExStart → Exchange → Loading → Full in seconds.
💘
Mental Note — say it once, own it forever
He was DOWN to INITiate a 2-WAY. They ExStart-ed to EXCHANGE stories, she kept LOADING up on the details — until she knew everything, and he was FULL of it. Hellos get you to 2-Way; databases get you to Full; the DR election decides whether you ever leave 2-Way at all.
