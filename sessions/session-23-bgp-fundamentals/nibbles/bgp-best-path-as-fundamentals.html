SESSION 23 · NIBBLE · 2-MINUTE REFERENCE

Steer Your Own, Request Someone Else’s

The one distinction that explains every BGP traffic-engineering knob you’ll ever touch.

Same attributes, opposite guarantees: one dial you own, one request you don't.

A 2-minute glance to reconstruct the whole outbound-vs-inbound mental model.

ONE CONCEPT · 2 MINUTES

Two Jobs, Two Different Guarantees

Your dial vs. your request — same BGP attributes, opposite guarantees.

JobAttributesWho decidesGuaranteed?
Steer your own outboundWeight, Local PreferenceYour own router / ASYes
Request influence on inboundAS-Path prepend, MEDThe neighboring ASNo — a request
Quick-check: "I want to choose which link MY traffic leaves through" → Weight / Local Pref. "I want to influence which link THEIR traffic arrives on" → AS-Path prepend / MED, and hope they honor it.
Exam trap: Local Preference influencing inbound traffic is always the wrong answer — it's stripped at the eBGP boundary and never reaches the neighboring AS.

MEMORY HOOK
A dial on your own dashboard vs. a paper airplane thrown over a fence. One you control completely. One you just hope lands well.
