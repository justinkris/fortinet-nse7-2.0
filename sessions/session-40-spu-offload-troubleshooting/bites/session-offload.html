NSE7 EF 7.6 · SESSION 40 · STUDY BITE

SPU Offloading, Sessions & Troubleshooting

Rapid mental model reactivation — assumes you already understand the topic.

The mental model in one paragraph: FortiGate offloads sessions to the NP7 after the first packet — the CPU installs a session key and steps aside. Whether a session is offloaded is visible in diagnose sys session list via the npu info: offload=X/Y field, where 9 = NP7 and 0 = CPU. When offload fails, the no_ofld_reason field immediately classifies the cause as either an architectural constraint (unfixable) or a configuration decision (reversible). Reading that field is always step one — before opening any other command.

The Command & Key Line

The verdict line — go here first
diagnose sys session list

npu info: flag=0x81/0x81, offload=9/9, ips_offload=0/0
                          ↑ forward / reverse — 9=NP7, 0=CPU

npu_state=0x000001 no_offload
no_ofld_reason:  disabled-by-policy
                 ↑ tells you constraint vs decision immediately

Offload Value Decode

offload valueMeaningStatus
9/9Both directions on NP7 — full fast pathOFFLOADED ✓
0/0Both directions on CPU — slow pathNOT OFFLOADED
9/0Asymmetric — investigate routingASYMMETRIC

Constraints vs Decisions

no_ofld_reasonTypeResponse
helperCONSTRAINTExpected. FTP/SIP/H.323. Do not chase. Move on.
software-tunnelCONSTRAINTExpected. GRE/SSL VPN. IPsec is the exception.
(session too short)CONSTRAINTDNS/ICMP complete before NP7 key is used. Normal.
disabled-by-profileDECISIONProxy UTM profile attached. Intentional? If not, switch to flow.
disabled-by-policyDECISIONauto-asic-offload explicitly disabled. Forgotten override. Fix it.
🧠
Primary Mental Model
Constraint = cannot be configured away. Decision = a human made a choice. The reason code tells you which in one glance — before you open any other command.

Troubleshooting Checklist

StepAction
1Read no_ofld_reason — constraint or decision?
2If helper or software-tunnel → stop. Expected behaviour.
3If disabled-by-profile → find policy, check proxy profile attachment
4If disabled-by-policy → find policy, remove set auto-asic-offload disable
5After fix: clear sessions, re-run list, confirm offload=9/9
Common mistakes: Treating no_ofld_reason: helper as a problem to fix. Confusing disabled-by-profile (proxy profile) with disabled-by-policy (explicit override) — different causes, different fixes. Interpreting offload=9/9 as a packet count.
Exam signal: "Regardless of policy configuration" in a question eliminates the two decisions and points to the three constraints — helper, software-tunnel, session-too-short.
Self-Test Questions — answer before looking at your notes
  1. A session entry shows offload=9/9. What does the number 9 represent, and what does the slash separate?
  2. You see no_ofld_reason: helper on a trading VLAN session. What do you do?
  3. What is the difference between disabled-by-profile and disabled-by-policy?
  4. Name three session types that cannot be NP7-offloaded regardless of policy configuration.
  5. A junior engineer asks why their DNS sessions show offload=0/0 but no reason code. What explains this?
