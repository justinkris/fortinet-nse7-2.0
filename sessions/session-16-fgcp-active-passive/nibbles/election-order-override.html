🍪 Study Nibble · <2 min · Session 16

The Election Waterfall & the Override Swap

Override doesn't turn priority on or off. It changes the order in which criteria are checked — it swaps the positions of priority and uptime. The election always stops at the first criterion with a clear winner.
Override DISABLED (default)
1
Most monitored interfaces up
2
Highest HA uptime
only decisive with a ≥5-minute lead
3
Highest priority
4
Highest serial number
always deterministic — no deadlock
Override ENABLED (CLI only)
1
Most monitored interfaces up
2
Highest priority
3
Highest HA uptime
≥5-minute lead required
4
Highest serial number
⇅ only rungs 2 & 3 swap — 1 and 4 never move
The trap: with override OFF, a primary that reboots and returns within 5 minutes still preempts — the uptime gap is indecisive, the election falls through to priority, and higher priority wins. Return after the survivor banks a 5-minute lead, and uptime decides first: the survivor keeps primary and priority is never consulted. Short absence = walk back in; long absence = door closed.
Override's price tag: a guaranteed second failover (and second NP7 resync) every time the preferred box recovers. Override + priority are per-member, never synced — set them on both boxes.
Want a manual failover?Do this
Override OFFdiagnose sys ha reset-uptime on the current primary — zeroes its clock, peer wins via uptime.
Override ONChange priorities — raise the secondary's or lower the primary's.
Uptime resets to 0 on: a reboot, OR a monitored-interface failure. It is HA uptime, not system uptime. Inspect: diagnose sys ha dump-by vcluster (member showing 0 = lowest; reset_cnt = times zeroed).
