==================
Extra Number: 04
Topic: Route Flapping & Route Flap Damping
Date: July 2026

WHY THIS EXTRA WAS CREATED
==========================
During Session 20, when asked to name the consequence of aggressive BGP timers at internet scale, the student guessed "routing loops." The actual answer — route flapping, countered by route flap damping — was new terminology, and the student explicitly requested a dedicated bite on it at the end of the session. This extra is that bite: a standalone side-quest reference, not a numbered teaching session.

CONCEPTS COVERED
================
- Route flapping: a route or session repeatedly going up/down; each flap = withdrawal + re-advertisement propagating to every router holding the prefix.
- Why a SESSION flap is categorically worse than a route flap: hold-timer expiry flushes every route from the peer, then the entire table (potentially 900k+ prefixes on internet core routers) re-advertises from scratch when the session returns.
- Route flap damping mechanism: per-prefix penalty added on each flap → suppress threshold (route unused and unadvertised even while "up") → exponential half-life decay (commonly 15 min) → reuse threshold → max suppress time ceiling.
- The design trade: damping deliberately sacrifices reachability (a suppressed route may be genuinely fine) to buy global stability — the same philosophy as BGP's slow default timers (keepalive 60s / hold 180s), expressed as per-prefix policy.
- Modern caveat: classic damping defaults proved too aggressive; legitimate convergence events could suppress innocent prefixes for up to an hour, so operator guidance (RIPE recommendations) shifted to disabling damping or using far gentler thresholds.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
=============
- Flapping causes update churn and control-plane CPU load — NOT routing loops. Loops are the distance-vector pathology; flapping is the instability-propagation pathology.
- Damping slows convergence for unstable prefixes on purpose. Any answer claiming damping "speeds up convergence" is a distractor.
- Anchor analogy: the boy who cried wolf, with a ledger — fines per false alarm, silence once fines pile up, forgiveness through decay, and a village that learned not to fine too harshly.

TROUBLESHOOTING LOGIC DISCUSSED
===============================
If a prefix is unreachable while the far-end operator swears it's up and advertised: check for suppression — damping will hold a route down even while the origin is healthy. If ALL routes from one peer vanished simultaneously: that's a session event (hold-timer expiry or TCP reset), not many individual withdrawals — investigate the session, not the prefixes.

EXAM-CRITICAL NOTES
===================
- Definitions of flapping vs damping, and the penalty/suppress/half-life/reuse vocabulary.
- Session flap = full-table withdraw + full-table re-advertise. This is the stated reason BGP tolerates 180s of silence while OSPF tolerates only 40s.
- Damping suppresses currently-reachable routes — reachability is the price of stability.
