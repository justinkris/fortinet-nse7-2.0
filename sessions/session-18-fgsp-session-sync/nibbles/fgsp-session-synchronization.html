STUDY NIBBLE · <2 MIN · SESSION 18

Structural Relay vs On-Demand Exception

Out of 1000 packets in a distributed session, how many actually cross between two firewalls? The answer depends entirely on which HA technology you're running — and that number is the single cleanest way to tell FGCP Active-Active and FGSP apart.

FGCP Active-Active

1000

Every single inbound packet of a distributed proxy session gets relayed from the primary to the secondary via 0x8891 — for the session's entire life, whether or not routing is ever actually asymmetric. The cost is baked in from the SYN. Permanent. Structural.

FGSP

1

Only the one packet that actually lands on the wrong peer gets handed back to the session owner — via L2 frame bounce or L3 UDP encapsulation. The other 999 never pay any cross-box cost at all. On-demand. An exception, not a tax.

Same goal — session continuity — completely different price tag
FGCP Active-ActiveFGSP
Relay triggerEvery inbound packet, alwaysOnly a genuinely misrouted packet
Mechanism0x8891 over the heartbeat linkL2 frame bounce, or L3 UDP encapsulation
Cost patternStructural — paid whether needed or notOn-demand — paid only when actually needed
Requires shared identity?Yes — one vMAC, one primaryNo — fully independent peers
Exam tell: "every inbound packet crosses a link, regardless of routing symmetry" → FGCP A-A. "A box occasionally forwards a packet to its peer only when routing goes sideways" → FGSP.
